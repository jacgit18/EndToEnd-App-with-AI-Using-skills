# Return to Play Progression

"Rest it a few more days and you'll probably be fine" is a calendar guess, not a progression
— the one thing that actually matters (can this tissue tolerate load *now*) never gets
checked before advancing. This skill forces a criteria-based walk through recovery: a phase
advances only when stated symptoms clear a bar, never on elapsed time alone, and a red flag
stops the walk instead of getting talked past.

## When this applies

Someone describes a soft-tissue or joint injury from training — a sprain, strain, a
"tweaked" joint, a mild ligament/tendon issue — and asks about resuming activity, adding
strength work back, or returning to their sport. Scoped to injuries at self-manageable
severity. Anything suggesting more (see Immediate red flags) exits to a doctor, not a phase.

## What this does not do

- **Diagnose or grade the injury.** No Grade 1/2/3 call, no fracture or complete-tear rule-out,
  no image reading. Reasons from stated symptoms; anything suggesting a more serious injury
  routes to a professional immediately rather than being talked through here.
- **Program a healthy athlete's training.** No injury present, no gate — a request for a
  workout plan, warm-up, or mobility routine is answered directly.
- **Replace physical therapy.** Names when a PT/doctor visit is warranted (see Escalation)
  instead of substituting for one indefinitely.

## Immediate red flags — stop, don't progress, refer out

Check these first. Any one present: recommend a doctor/urgent care now, do not proceed to
phase-gating below.

- Visible deformity, or the joint looks wrong compared to the other side.
- Complete inability to bear any weight or use the limb, not just pain with use.
- Severe, rapidly-worsening swelling — beyond the ordinary swelling of a sprain.
- A felt "pop" at the moment of injury plus immediate severe pain and loss of function
  (possible complete tear).
- Numbness, tingling, or loss of pulse/color distal to the injury (possible nerve or
  vascular involvement).

None of these present → the injury is in the self-manageable range this skill covers.

## Inputs the walk needs — ask, don't invent

1. **What's injured and the mechanism** — which joint/structure, what happened (a specific
   movement, a direct hit, a chronic ache that flared).
2. **Time since injury.**
3. **Current symptoms against function**, not just a pain number — can they walk/move
   normally, how much swelling, what specific movement or load still hurts.
4. **What they're returning to** — the actual sport-specific demand (grappling pressure
   through the joint, kicking, stance switching, sprinting, overhead load). This is what
   Phase 4 has to eventually load, not just "feels okay generally."

A phase call made without these is a guess dressed as an assessment.

## The four-phase gate

Each phase has entry criteria. **Advancing because "it's been X days" without the criteria
met is exactly the failure this skill exists to stop** — the windows below are typical
ranges, not permission; stated symptoms decide, not the calendar.

### Phase 1 — Protection (pain & swelling reduction)
**Here if:** normal walking/use still hurts, the injured motion is painful, swelling present.
**Do:** relative rest, protective gear if relevant (taping, a stiff shoe, a brace),
pain-free range of motion only. **No strengthening yet.**
**Typical window:** days for a mild sprain/strain, 2–3 weeks for a moderate one — criteria
decide when to move on, not this range.

### Phase 2 — Early activation
**Advance only when ALL are true:** normal walking/use is pain-free · swelling is minimal ·
a small range of motion is pain-free · tenderness is clearly decreasing (not just "a little
better").
**Do:** low-load isometric and light-band strengthening of the specific structure, not a
whole-body session.

### Phase 3 — Load the mechanism
**Advance only when ALL are true:** the Phase 2 exercises are pain-free at increased
reps/load · the joint bears normal single-limb stance without guarding · moderate range of
motion under light load is pain-free.
**Do:** progressive loading toward the structure's actual function (single-leg calf raises
for a toe/ankle, controlled sled work, split-stance loading). **This is the phase most
people rush — and rushing it is the most common reinjury point.**

### Phase 4 — Return to sport-specific force
**Advance only when ALL are true:** full-intensity Phase 3 loading is pain-free · the joint
tolerates the *specific* demand named in Input 4 at low intensity (light plyometrics, a
shadow rep, a slow version of the sport movement) without symptoms.
**Do:** ramp the actual sport-specific pattern — shadow work before contact, light technical
work before full pressure, gradual reintroduction of the specific stress rather than
jumping straight back into full training.

**Under-rehabbing tell:** stopping at generic strengthening (Phase 2/3 exercises alone)
without ever loading the actual sport-specific pattern named in Input 4 is incomplete — the
structure has never been tested against the demand that will actually be placed on it.

## Reading pain correctly — the distinction that gets skipped

- **Mild discomfort during the exercise that doesn't worsen** → generally acceptable at the
  current phase.
- **Pain the next morning, not during the activity itself** → the tissue did not tolerate
  that load. The single most-missed signal — regress to the prior phase's load, don't hold
  steady and hope it settles.
- **Sharp pain, or pain on a bone/joint rather than the soft tissue being worked** → stop
  that exercise entirely, don't just lighten it.
- **Numbness or tingling during any manual therapy or exercise** (rolling, banded work,
  loading) → stop immediately — a nerve-compression signal, not a push-through signal.

## Output block

```
Injury:               <structure/joint> — <mechanism> — <days since injury>
Red flags:            <none found | STOP — refer to doctor/urgent care: reason>
Current function:     <walking/use, swelling, ROM, tenderness — as stated>
Phase:                <1 Protection | 2 Early activation | 3 Load the mechanism | 4 Return to sport>
Criteria met:         <each phase-entry criterion, pass/fail — never "seems fine">
Next gate:            <what has to be true to advance, or "advance now" if all criteria met>
Sport-specific target: <the demand from Input 4 — what Phase 4 must eventually load>
Escalation:           <none | recommend PT/doctor — reason>
```

## Escalation — when this stops being self-manageable

- No improvement, or a new plateau, past the typical window for the stated severity.
- A second occurrence of the same injury (a genuine reinjury, not lingering soreness).
- A joint injury (versus pure muscle/tendon soreness) not clearly improving phase-to-phase.
- The person explicitly wants a professional-graded return-to-play clearance (common ahead
  of a real competition return).

Name the referral rather than continuing to generate home phases once one of these is true.

## Red flags — the walk isn't done

- A phase-advance recommendation with no criteria actually checked against stated symptoms.
- "You're probably fine by now" based on elapsed time alone, no function check.
- Next-morning pain treated the same as during-exercise pain (see Reading pain correctly).
- Phase 4 waved through without naming the actual sport-specific demand from Input 4.
- A red-flag symptom (deformity, total inability to bear weight, numbness) talked past
  instead of routed to a doctor immediately.

## Portability

Repo-agnostic. Writes nothing; produces the output block in chat. Copy the
`return-to-play-progression/` directory into another repo's `.claude/skills/` to use it
there.
